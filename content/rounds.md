## What the next rounds probably look like

One comparable public report (a Data Center Controls Engineer candidate, Glassdoor, Mar 2026) described the loop as recruiter screen → technical → behavioral → hiring manager → final with a VP, CEO or COO. In that report:

- **Technical** — about 45 minutes with a fellow engineer; casual; questions about past experience plus scenario-based technical questions.
- **Behavioral** — about 30 minutes with a senior manager; built around "your most difficult challenge", with many follow-ups drilling into the answer.
- **Hiring manager** — about 30 minutes; a blend, including how you work fast and under pressure.
- **Final** — executive; the candidate guessed the decision was mostly made before it.

Brandon's hiring manager round came first, so the working assumption is: **Round 2 = peer technical** (primary focus of this update), **Round 3 = behavioral drill-down** (secondary), **Round 4 = executive** (not covered). One report is thin evidence; the order varies by role.

## What every round is testing

Fluidstack says its operating principles are what interviews really test for. Paraphrased:

- **"Be a barrel"** — full autonomy and end-to-end ownership; take on scope without being asked.
- **Extreme urgency** — move everything forward as fast as possible.
- **First principles** — challenge assumptions; no reasoning by analogy, no egos; best idea wins.
- **Love of the game** — the AI frontier is the problem worth working on; long hours at high intensity.
- **Build something that matters.**

The story-to-value map in `story-scripts.md` ("Which story answers which question") already ties each story to these.
